How to win

Be the first team to have all your words covered. Your spymaster knows which ones they are. You don't.

Find them allYour team wins when none of your words are left, even if the other team guessed your last one.
Or they hit the assassinIf the other team touches the assassin, you win on the spot.
Before you playRulebook p. 2–3

Setup

  1. 1Split into 2 teamsRoughly even, at least 2 players each.
  2. 2Pick a spymaster per teamBoth spymasters sit on the same side, opposite their operatives.
  3. 3Deal 25 wordsRandom cards in a 5 × 5 grid.
  4. 4Draw a random key cardSlide it into the stand between the spymasters. Any side up. Only spymasters look at it.
  5. 5Hand out the tiles8 red to the red spymaster, 8 blue to the blue one. 7 bystanders and the assassin go in between.
  6. 6Give the double agent to the starting teamThe triangles on the key's edge show who starts. They have 1 extra word, so they flip the double agent to their color.
When it's your teamRulebook p. 4–5

A turn

Your spymaster gives 1 clue. Your operatives touch words one at a time until they miss or stop.

Spymaster One clue

Say 1 word and 1 number. The word links to your team's words, and the number says how many. Example: tree: 2 for ROOT and BARK.

Operatives Guess

  1. 1Talk it over, then touch a cardThe spymaster covers it with the tile color the key shows.
  2. 2Your color?You may guess again.
  3. 3Stop any timeAfter at least 1 guess.
Plus oneYou can make up to 1 more guess than the clue's number, as long as you keep hitting your color. Use it to catch a word you missed on an earlier clue.

What you can sayRulebook p. 6–7

Valid clues

The clue must be about meaning. Not sure? Quietly ask the other spymaster. If they allow it, it's valid.

  • No letters or positions: "b: 3" for BED, BUG and BOW is out. Letters and numbers are fine if they mean something, like x: 1 for RAY.
  • No word on the table: no form of a visible word, and no part of a visible compound word (no horse while HORSESHOE is uncovered).
  • English only, unless your group would use the foreign word in an English sentence.
  • No accents or melodies as hints.
  • Soundalikes: only if about meaning (snail for MAIL is fine). Your group can agree to allow all soundalikes.
  • Same spelling counts as one word, so lead works for CAPTAIN and GOLD. Spell your clue if asked.
  • Names: one-word names are always fine. Your group can allow longer names, titles and abbreviations.
Invalid clueThe team's turn ends with no guesses, and the other spymaster may cover one of their own words. The other team can choose to let it stand. If nobody notices until later, it counts.
Keep it fairRulebook p. 8

Table manners

  • Spymasters keep a straight face. Don't react to the discussion or reach for a tile until a word is touched.
  • No extra info. Not even "this is a stretch".
  • Don't remind operatives about words they missed on earlier clues, unless they ask you to repeat a clue.
  • Operatives don't watch the spymaster for reactions.
Once you're comfortableRulebook p. 7

Expert rules

  • Unlimited clue: say "unlimited" instead of a number. Operatives can guess as long as they keep hitting their color.
  • Zero clue: "feathers: 0" means "avoid the feathers word". Also allows unlimited guesses.
